1 · CRT (Candle Range Theory)

{{ strategies.crt.evidence }}

What counts as a setup (CRT)

  1. Range candle: a finished D1 or H4 candle on Exness server time (GMT+0). Its high, low and 50% level are the range.
  2. Sweep: the next candle (or the one after, if the middle candle stayed inside) trades beyond one side (past the range candle's high or low) and closes back inside the range candle's body, between its open and close; a close that is only inside the wick doesn't count. Sweeping both sides doesn't count either.
  3. Trigger on a lower timeframe (D1 → H1 or M15; H4 → M15 or M5): a CISD (close beyond the open of the run of candles that made the sweep) or an MSS (close beyond the last swing before the sweep). The setup is armed until then, for up to two range candles. If price goes past the sweep extreme first, it is cancelled.

Your CRT rules: what gets recommended

  • Trigger in the London (07:00–16:00 London) or New York (08:00–17:00 New York) session, not Friday after 15:00 New York.
  • With the daily direction (last daily close beyond a swing high = up, beyond a swing low = down), or against it only when the sweep ran into an older higher-timeframe order block or FVG on the left.
  • At least 1 : 3 reward to risk after spread.

Setups that fail a rule are still listed, collapsed, so you can see what the rules removed.

Entry options

  • Entries: market at the trigger close; limit at the lower-timeframe FVG (edge or middle); limit at the order block (open or middle). Order blocks count only if they took liquidity and left an FVG.
  • Stops, sized for the spread: beyond the sweep extreme plus 2 spreads (default), or plus 0.1 ATR; the tight FVG/OB stop is shown only when the spread is under 15% of the risk.
  • Targets: the opposite side of the range or the next swing beyond it, all at once or half off at the 50% level with the stop moved to entry.
  • Order rules: an unfilled limit is dropped when the second range candle after the sweep candle closes (D1 about 2 days, H4 about 8 hours), or earlier if price reaches the 50% level first. Tested (v13): fills after price already reached 50% lose about −0.4R each, so the cancel stays.
  • Ranking: by each option's 2021–2024 average result, shrunk towards the overall average for small samples. Sample size is always shown.

Shown as checklist, not as filters

Killzone, FVG and order block present, both CISD and MSS, mid-timeframe MSS, HTF MSS, MSnR level, sweep candle size and depth. None of them changed results versus chance in any test from 2021 to 2026, so they are information for your judgment, not rules.

Market structure (shared by the order block, Toni and Lingard)

Rebuilt 27 Nov 2026 after your drawing review. Only the main (external) swings count:

  • BOS = a break of the high (or low) of the current leg, once a pullback has fixed it (2 lower highs after it).
  • MSS = a break of the protected swing: the higher low (or lower high) that launched the last BOS. "A break is a break": spike wicks and news candles count.
  • Small swings inside a leg are internal structure and are ignored. Trend = the direction of the last BOS/MSS (D1 and H4 for bias).
  • Order block (your definition) = a 2-candle engulfing, or candle + pin bar + engulfing, where the last candle closes beyond the bodies of the one or two before it; the box is drawn from the first candle.

2 · Order block (H1 order block · D1+H4 trend)

Evidence, honestly: {{ strategies.smc_ob.evidence }} Results: Performance.

  1. Trend: D1 and H4 external trends point the same way.
  2. MSS on H1 in that direction (body close beyond the protected swing).
  3. Order block: your 2/3-candle block at the top (sells) or bottom (buys) of the move the MSS came from, complete by the MSS candle.
  4. Entry: limit at the first candle's near edge, valid 12 H1 candles. Stop: beyond the block / the move's extreme + 2 spreads. Target: 3R. Closed after 5 trading days if neither is hit.

3 · IRL/ERL (IRL → ERL · H1 order block)

Evidence, honestly: {{ strategies.irl_erl.evidence }} Results: Performance.

Price moves from internal range liquidity to external range liquidity and back. On H4: the dealing range is the external high and low; ERL = the stops beyond them; IRL = unfilled fair value gaps inside. The last event sets the next draw: took the range high → down to IRL; took the range low → up to IRL; traded into a bullish FVG → up to the range high; into a bearish FVG → down to the range low.

  1. Bias: the H4 IRL/ERL draw points the same way as the D1 external trend.
  2. MSS on H1 in that direction (body close beyond the protected swing).
  3. Order block: your 2/3-candle block at the top/bottom of the move, complete by the MSS candle.
  4. Entry: limit at the first candle's near edge, valid 12 H1 candles. Stop: beyond the block + 2 spreads. Target: 3R. Closed after 5 trading days.

Same entry as the order-block strategy; only the bias differs (IRL/ERL instead of the D1 + H4 trend). Order-block cards also show the IRL/ERL state for comparison.

4 · Toni Iyke (structure → inducement → POI)

Evidence, honestly: {{ strategies.toni.evidence }} Official videos and paid-class notes merged into one strategy (the classes are the detailed version).

  1. Trend: D1 external trend.
  2. Structure on H1 with body closes: an MSS (reversal) or a multiple BOS (one leg closes beyond at least 2 swing levels). A single BOS is ignored.
  3. Inducement: the first pullback after the structure candle, confirmed when a later candle body-closes beyond the structure candle's close.
  4. POI, his 4 rules: breakers (the bodies of the levels broken) or your order block at the origin; not touched by the inducement leg; the one closest to the inducement.
  5. Entry: limit at the POI edge (order block: wick end if the wick is shorter than the body, else the body). Stop: beyond the POI + 2 spreads. Target: the swing point (last high/low before the POI is reached). Valid 20 H1 candles; closed after 5 days.

5 · Lingard (first-pullback liquidity → order block)

Evidence, honestly: {{ strategies.lingard.evidence }}

  1. Trend: D1 external trend (stands in for his monthly/weekly top-down).
  2. Structure on H4 with wick breaks (his rule). After an MSS, the first BOS in the same direction makes its pullback the first-pullback liquidity.
  3. POI: your 2/3-candle order block just beyond that liquidity (within 1.5 ATR), inside the move that started the trend.
  4. Entry: limit at its near edge, in London or New York hours. Stop: beyond the block + 2 spreads. Target: 5R (his minimum). Valid 20 H4 candles; closed after 10 days.

Not automated: his monthly → weekly top-down, breakers, fake zones and the lower-timeframe confirmation entry. Use the review tags for those.

6 · Ore's model (H4 POI → M15 liquidity sweep)

Evidence, honestly: {{ strategies.ore_poi.evidence }} Report: v25 and v26 on Backtest.

  1. Bias: D1 external trend. Trades only that way.
  2. Higher-timeframe POI: price makes its top (sells) or bottom (buys) inside a fresh H4 order block (your 2/3-candle block) or H4 fair value gap, on the zone's first tap. H4 breaker blocks are ignored: an order block that failed is skipped and the scanner waits for the next order block.
  3. Entry model on M15 (wick breaks, "a break is a break"): an MSS, then a BOS the same way. The BOS pullback is the liquidity (the fake move that traps early traders); the entry sits beyond it.
  4. Entry zone: the M15 breaker at the swing the MSS broke, if there is one; otherwise your order block at the top/bottom. The pattern must finish within 30 M15 candles of the top/bottom (it should come at the start of the move).
  5. Order: limit at the zone's near edge, valid 30 M15 candles. Stop: beyond the zone + 2 spreads. Target: 3R. Closed after 5 days.

Tracking shows: the D1 trend; the nearest untouched H4 zone and how far price is from it; once it's tapped, the M15 pattern it is waiting for.
