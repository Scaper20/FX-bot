What the backtests showed

1-minute Dukascopy data, Nov 2021 – Nov 2026, spread included (GBP/USD 1.0 pip, gold $0.35). Every rule set was written down and committed before it was run. On 20 Nov 2026 you chose to use all years, so the later evidence is year-by-year consistency, walk-forwards and markets never used to build a rule. Full reports: backtest/reports in the repository.

Bottom line: no fixed rule tested here has an edge that holds on years or markets it wasn't built on. What's left untested is your own judgment, which My test measures on live setups.

1. The CRT pattern hits targets at the chance rate

2,550 CRTs from 2021–2023, entered at the sweep candle close with the stop at the sweep extreme, no costs:

Target reached before the stopActualChance
50% level57.9%57.0%
Opposite side of the range41.8%41.7%

Chance means: on a random path, the probability of reaching the target before the stop is risk ÷ (risk + reward). The same holds in every split tested (session, direction, close depth, sweep size, timeframe, pair, side).

The edge-hunt "pass" buys at the New York close, where our bid-only data dips about 1.3 pips while spreads widen. Real rollover spreads and swap cost more than the 1-pip gain. Checking it needs EUR/USD ask-price data. Pure luck also passes this walk-forward about 1 time in 8.

4. Why it loses

  • With no edge, every trade loses about the spread. With tight M5 stops, the spread is half the risk on GBP/USD and 80% on gold.
  • The 1 : 3 rule selects the weakest CRTs: closes right at the swept edge and tiny sweeps.
  • Limit entries miss the moves that work: 24% are cancelled because price ran to the 50% level without coming back.
  • Trying many versions always turns up a few that look good by luck. That's why every good-looking result had to pass on years or markets it wasn't built on, and none did.
